# Edith's Last Call: story bible, entity bible and beat sheet

(Working title was "Wellness Check"; that name is taken on Steam. The project folder keeps the old name.)

One-sentence hook: you are a deputy sent to check on an old woman in a rotting farmhouse at the end of a forest road, and the only way
out is to finish the job she has been waiting months for someone living to do, while she begs you not to.

## Story bible

1. **Who is the protagonist and what brings him here?** Deputy Cole Brandt, Ashby County Sheriff's Office, overnight shift, alone.
   Dispatch sends him up County Road 9 for a wellness check on Edith Harrow, 86, who lives alone and has not been seen in months.
2. **What does he expect to do?** Knock, see that she is alive, call it in. Ten minutes.
3. **Why this place, why tonight?** Somebody phoned dispatch, gave the address, said "she needs company" and hung up. Later the trace
   comes back: the call was placed from the Harrow landline, whose cord has been cut for over a year.
4. **Why can he not just leave or call for help?** Half a minute after he walks in, the front door shuts behind him and will not open.
   Every other door and window was nailed shut from the inside long ago. His radio, fine in the car, gives static in the house.
5. **What happened before he arrived?** Edith's son Daniel died in Vietnam in March 1969; she never accepted it ("He is only late").
   Her husband Walter died at home in 1989. Her daughter Ruth, worn down, stopped coming. Edith set four places at the table every
   night for nineteen years. Dementia came: the notes she pinned up for herself get shorter and stranger. The county sent a home aide,
   Mrs. Pruitt, who moved into the upstairs back room, strapped Edith in at night on the doctor's order, and fled after seven nights
   because something heavy walked the hall and asked her, in Edith's voice, to stay. In her grandmother's book Edith had found a way to
   ask for company: tie a knot of your own hair for each one who left, and ask. On the 9th something came. The pact was "never let me
   be alone again", and it was answered literally: it made room in her. She grew too long for her nightgown and her doorways, the
   white went out of her left eye, and her hands could no longer close on scissors. She went down to the cellar, pulled the preserve
   shelf across the root cellar "while she still fit", and died in her chair. The power was cut off for non-payment in September.
   The lights still burn.
6. **What does the entity want, and why him?** Not to be alone. She does not want him dead; she wants him to stay for supper, for ever.
   Underneath, what is left of Edith wants to be let go, and made the call herself.
7. **What changes his understanding?** The table set for four; the cut phone cord; the calendar ("Nobody. Nobody. SOMEONE CAME."); her
   reminders to herself; the doctor's letter; the aide's chart and notebook; her diaries; Ruth's letter; Danny's last letter and the
   telegram; the dolls in the sewing room; the last page of the book in her hand; the note in the cellar that tells him exactly what to
   bring and warns him not to listen when she asks him to stay. About 55 objects can also be examined for a line of what he notices.
8. **What must the player learn or do?** Find what was theirs: Ruth's music box (under her bed), Walter's ring (with the knots), Daniel's
   dog tags (his footlocker in the attic). Find something to cut with (the sewing shears). Find her behind the shelf. Lay the keepsakes
   at her feet and cut the three knots. On the way: the brass key (kitchen drawer) opens her sitting room, which holds the iron key
   (prayer room) and the desk key; Walter's desk holds his keys to the upstairs back rooms; Daniel's room holds the attic key; the
   footlocker holds the cellar padlock key; a blown fuse (spare in the den, box in the pantry) brings the lights back.
9. **How does it end?** As the third knot goes she is in his face, screaming "STAY". Then quiet, and "thank you". It is morning. The
   doors are open. At the cruiser he calls it in, and dispatch tells him another call is coming in on the emergency line: the Harrow
   landline, again.

Cast: Deputy Brandt (close, low, tired; he speaks to dispatch and when she is on him, and otherwise his thoughts are on-screen text),
Dispatch (radio, clipped), Edith (thin, slow, phone / whisper / in the room). Ruth, Walter, Daniel and Mrs. Pruitt exist only on paper.

## The house

A Victorian farmhouse that was once the best house on the road: butter-yellow clapboard, white trim, green shutters, a wraparound
porch with turned posts, an entry gable, a bay window, a round side turret, dormers, brick chimneys, a lower rear wing. Fourteen months
of nobody: boards gone, shutters hanging, a rusted tin roof, brambles, a clothes line with last year's washing, the family plot under
the willow. It stands at the end of 320 m of dirt track through pine forest.

Ground floor: front hall, living room, dining room, kitchen, pantry (fuse box), her sitting room (locked), Walter's den, downstairs
bath, the sickroom where she slept at the end, rear hall and the nailed back door. Upstairs: master bedroom, Ruth's room (locked until
S4), bath, the prayer room (locked), and behind a locked hall door Daniel's room (kept exactly as it was), the sewing room that was
going to be a nursery, the aide's room, a second bath. Attic. Cellar and the root cellar behind the shelf.

## Entity bible: Edith

- **Silhouette:** an old woman drawn out to about nine feet, always stooped because rooms are too small for her; arms that hang past
  her knees, long fingers, bare feet, a stained sleeveless nightgown gone short on her. Grey hair hangs over the right half of her
  face; on the left, one huge lidless eye that is black all through, with only a thin pale ring of iris round the pupil, and that
  turns to follow you.
- **Movement:** she lurches, hips swaying, arms dangling like weights (Walk). When she comes fast her arms are out in front (Run) or
  she is down on all fours (Crawl). At rest she breathes and sways and her head ticks (Idle). She sits (the kitchen table, the rocking
  chair). She only stands to her full height twice (Rise / Tall). All of it is skeletal animation on a 24-bone rig.
- **Face:** never still. The jaw hangs a little and works when she speaks; the brow creeps down over the eye; one side of the
  upper lip twitches up off black teeth. When she screams the mouth opens far too long, down to her collar bone.
- **Sound:** her feet first: bare, heavy, wet, on boards that creak under her weight. A wet, rattling breath; the scream; a short
  hurt shriek when she is driven off. Her voice now is many voices at once, some far too low, a little out of step with each other.
  Only in the memories does she sound like one old woman. Around the house, quietly: a laugh in another room, crying behind a wall,
  humming, a whisper at the ear. The house answers her: a floorboard under weight, a switch clicking off, a door that was shut.
- **Traces:** scratches at door height and above; pencil marks up a door frame; the places set at the table; lights that go out;
  flies.
- **Rules (kept consistent):**
  1. She is bound to the house and cannot follow past the porch.
  2. She cannot be killed. But she cannot stand gunfire: the flash and the noise make her flinch, and four or five shots drive her
     off for a few minutes. Cartridges are few. (The first time, in the long hall, she takes the flinches and keeps coming.)
  3. She does not kill. When she reaches you she puts you on the floor and lets go: she wants you kept, not dead. The third time she
     catches you, and in the cellar if you take too long, she keeps you (fail state, checkpoint).
  4. Her nearness kills light: the house bulbs go out, your flashlight dips, flickers or dies.
  5. She is strongest where she is tied: the three knots. Cut them where she lies and she is released.
- **Staged reveal:** nothing for eight to ten minutes but the house (door, thumps, steps overhead, the rocking chair, a light going
  out, scratching, the radio) -> half a face at a doorway -> the whole of her walking the long hall -> her at the table -> her full
  height in the dark -> only her arm, from under a bed -> in the rocking chair -> crawling in the attic -> sprinting the hall -> face
  to face in the cellar.  Alongside, for anyone who looks: an eye in a hole in the wall, hair hanging from the rafters.

## Entity bible: the Company

- **What it is:** what answered her on the ninth night. It has no name; in her notes and drawings it is "my friend", "the Company",
  "him". It gave her exactly what she asked for, never to be alone, by moving in.
- **Look:** a standing black outline about nine feet tall with no face, no features and no light on it at all, like a hole cut in
  the room. It never moves while it is looked at and it never comes closer.
- **Behaviour:** it is seen at most four times, only after the first sight of Edith, at the far end of a room or hall. When the
  light finds it the light stutters, and the corner is empty. It does not attack; Edith does that. It watches what it owns.
- **Her drawings:** Edith has gone back to being a child. Crayon drawings on her sitting-room wall and around the house show a
  small figure in a nightgown holding hands with a tall black one, the two of them at supper ("SUPPER FOR 2"), and in a
  child's letters "HE NEVER LEVES". It is the only one who did not leave her.
- **The memories:** each of the first three times she has hold of him he sees one, in order: the last Thanksgiving, her
  family going out of the front door while she asks them to stay; the attic, where she finds her grandmother's book under
  Danny's things; the ninth night, the candles, and the tall black shape in the corner. He later finds the same book in the attic.
